# AI Startup Pitch Deck Template
## Optimized for Pre-Seed/Angel Investors

---

## Deck Structure (15-18 slides recommended)

This template is designed specifically for AI startups targeting pre-seed and angel investors, with emphasis on technical credibility balanced with business viability.

---

### Slide 1: Cover
**Elements:**
- Company name and logo
- Tagline (one compelling sentence)
- Your name and title
- Date
- Confidential notice (optional)

**Example Tagline:**
"Building the AI mediation layer for [specific use case]"

---

### Slide 2: The Problem
**Goal:** Make the investor feel the pain

**Content:**
- 2-3 specific pain points your target customer faces
- Quantify the problem (time lost, money wasted, inefficiency)
- Use a customer quote or statistic if available

**For AI Startups:**
- Focus on technical pain points that require AI solutions
- Show why traditional software approaches fail
- Emphasize urgency (why now?)

**Example:**
"Enterprise teams waste 40% of their time on [X] because existing tools rely on [outdated approach]. Current solutions fail to [specific limitation], costing companies $[amount] annually."

---

### Slide 3: The Solution
**Goal:** Show your AI innovation clearly

**Content:**
- Your product in one sentence
- How it specifically solves the problems from Slide 2
- Your unique AI approach or technology
- Visual: product screenshot, diagram, or demo GIF

**For AI Startups:**
- Explain the AI/ML component without overwhelming jargon
- Show the "magic moment" - what your AI enables that wasn't possible before
- Balance technical depth with accessibility

**Example:**
"[Company Name] uses [specific AI technique] to automatically [key functionality], reducing [problem] by [X%] and enabling [new capability]."

---

### Slide 4: Product Demo / How It Works
**Goal:** Show don't tell

**Content:**
- 3-4 annotated screenshots showing user workflow
- OR: Simple flowchart showing data → AI → output
- Keep it simple - investors aren't users yet

**For AI Startups:**
- Show input → AI processing → output
- Highlight where AI adds intelligence
- Demonstrate the experience, not the algorithm

**Visual Example:**
1. User inputs [X]
2. AI analyzes using [technology]
3. System outputs [Y] in real-time
4. User achieves [outcome]

---

### Slide 5: Market Size
**Goal:** Prove this is a big enough opportunity

**Content:**
- TAM (Total Addressable Market): The entire market universe
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): Portion you can reach
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): What you can realistically capture in 3-5 years

**For AI Startups:**
- Reference AI market growth projections
- Cite industry analyst reports (Gartner, McKinsey, etc.)
- Show market tailwinds (AI adoption rates, regulatory drivers)

**Example:**
- TAM: $50B enterprise software market
- SAM: $10B AI-powered [category] market
- SOM: $500M target segment (specific vertical/use case)
- Growing at 35% CAGR (AI-specific growth rate)

**Sources to cite:**
- Gartner Magic Quadrants
- IDC market reports
- McKinsey AI studies

---

### Slide 6: Traction
**Goal:** Show momentum and validation

**Content:** (Pick your strongest 3-4 metrics)
- Revenue: MRR, ARR, or total revenue
- Users: Active users, signups, waitlist
- Engagement: DAU/MAU, retention rate, usage frequency
- Pilots/POCs: Number of pilot customers, conversion rate
- Partnerships: Strategic partnerships or integrations
- Press/Recognition: Notable coverage or awards

**For AI Startups (Pre-Seed Stage):**
If you don't have revenue yet, show:
- Beta users and their feedback
- Model performance metrics (accuracy, speed improvements)
- Technical milestones (model trained, API launched)
- Design partners or LOIs (letters of intent)

**Example:**
- "10 pilot customers, 8 converting to paid (80% conversion)"
- "AI model achieves 95% accuracy, 3x faster than baseline"
- "12,000 waitlist signups in 6 weeks"
- "$15K MRR, growing 40% month-over-month"

---

### Slide 7: Business Model
**Goal:** Show how you make money

**Content:**
- Revenue model: SaaS subscription, usage-based, enterprise licensing, etc.
- Pricing tiers (if applicable)
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV) if you have data
- Sales strategy: Self-serve, inside sales, or enterprise field sales

**For AI Startups:**
- Consider usage-based pricing (per API call, per prediction, etc.)
- Show unit economics if available
- Explain how you'll monetize the AI layer

**Example:**
"SaaS subscription model:
- Starter: $99/month (up to 10 users)
- Professional: $499/month (up to 50 users)
- Enterprise: Custom pricing (unlimited users + dedicated support)

Target CAC: $500 | Target LTV: $5,000 (10:1 LTV:CAC)"

---

### Slide 8: Competition / Why Now
**Goal:** Show market landscape and your differentiation

**Content:**
**Option A: Competitive Matrix**
- X-axis: One key dimension (e.g., ease of use)
- Y-axis: Another dimension (e.g., AI sophistication)
- Plot competitors and position yourself in the ideal quadrant

**Option B: Feature Comparison Table**
- List 3-4 competitors
- Show 5-6 key features/capabilities
- Highlight where you're superior

**PLUS: Why Now?**
- What changed to make this possible now?
- Technology enablers (e.g., LLMs, compute costs, open-source models)
- Market shifts (e.g., AI adoption, regulatory changes)

**For AI Startups:**
- Competitors: incumbent software + other AI startups
- Differentiation: Model performance, data advantage, user experience, speed, cost
- "Why Now" is critical - explain the AI/technical breakthrough

**Example Why Now:**
"Foundation models (GPT-4, Claude) make [X] possible for the first time. Enterprise AI adoption grew 80% in 2025. Our approach leverages [new technique] released in 2024."

---

### Slide 9: Go-to-Market Strategy
**Goal:** Show you know how to acquire customers

**Content:**
- Target customer profile (persona, company size, role)
- Channels: How you'll reach them
- Initial wedge: How you'll get first 100 customers
- Expansion plan: How you'll scale

**For AI Startups:**
- Often product-led growth (PLG) with free tier or trial
- Technical audiences (engineers, data scientists) require different channels
- Community building (GitHub, Discord, developer advocates)

**Example:**
"Phase 1 (Months 1-12): PLG + Outbound
- Target: AI/ML teams at Series A-C startups (100-500 employees)
- Channels: Product Hunt launch, dev community partnerships, cold outreach
- Goal: 50 paying customers

Phase 2 (Year 2): Expand to Enterprise
- Target: Fortune 1000 AI initiatives
- Channels: Field sales, partnerships with cloud providers
- Goal: $1M+ contracts"

---

### Slide 10: Team
**Goal:** Prove you're the right team to execute

**Content:**
- Founders: Name, title, 1-2 sentence background
- Headshots (professional, approachable)
- Relevant credentials: Prior companies, exits, technical expertise, domain knowledge
- Key advisors (if notable)

**For AI Startups:**
- Technical credibility is CRITICAL
- Highlight: PhDs, AI research, ML engineering at top companies (OpenAI, Google Brain, DeepMind)
- Show domain expertise in target industry
- Balance technical + business/GTM skills

**Example:**
"Jane Doe, CEO
- Previously ML Engineer at Google Brain (3 years)
- Published 5 papers on [AI subfield]
- MBA from Stanford

John Smith, CTO
- PhD in Computer Science, MIT
- Built AI systems at [recognizable company]
- 10+ years in NLP and generative AI"

---

### Slide 11: Traction Timeline (Optional but Recommended)
**Goal:** Show rapid progress and momentum

**Content:**
- Visual timeline of key milestones achieved
- Dates + accomplishments
- Show acceleration

**Example:**
- Jan 2025: Founded, started model development
- Apr 2025: First prototype, 3 design partners
- Jul 2025: Beta launch, 500 signups
- Oct 2025: 50 paying customers, $10K MRR
- Jan 2026: 150 customers, $25K MRR, raising pre-seed

---

### Slide 12: Roadmap (Optional)
**Goal:** Show where you're going with the funding

**Content:**
- Next 12-18 months of planned features/milestones
- Tied to funding ask
- Realistic but ambitious

**For AI Startups:**
- Model improvements (accuracy, latency, cost)
- New AI capabilities or use cases
- Integrations and partnerships
- Enterprise features

**Example:**
"With funding, next 12 months:
- Q2 2026: Launch v2 with [feature], improve model accuracy to 98%
- Q3 2026: Enterprise tier, SOC2 compliance
- Q4 2026: API marketplace launch, 5 integration partners
- Q1 2027: $100K MRR, 300 customers"

---

### Slide 13: Financials (Optional for Pre-Seed)
**Goal:** Show you understand unit economics

**Content (if you have it):**
- Revenue projections (12-18 months)
- Key cost drivers
- Burn rate and runway
- Path to profitability or next milestone

**For Pre-Seed AI Startups:**
- Often too early for detailed financials
- Can show: assumed CAC, LTV, gross margin
- Explain burn: team (X%), infrastructure/compute (Y%), GTM (Z%)

**If including:**
"Projected financials:
- Q4 2026: $50K MRR
- Q4 2027: $200K MRR
- Burn: $40K/month (3 FTEs + $10K compute)
- Runway: 18 months with this raise"

---

### Slide 14: The Ask
**Goal:** Be crystal clear about what you need

**Content:**
- How much you're raising
- Round structure (SAFE, priced round, etc.)
- Valuation/cap (if you're sharing)
- Use of funds (pie chart or bullets)
- Target close date

**Example:**
"Raising $500K pre-seed round
- Structure: SAFE, $5M cap
- Use of funds:
  - 60% Team (hire 2 engineers)
  - 25% Product development & AI infrastructure
  - 15% Go-to-market & customer acquisition
- Timeline: Closing in 6 weeks (mid-March 2026)
- Already committed: $200K (40%)"

**For AI Startups:**
- Allocate budget for compute/infrastructure (AWS, GCP credits)
- Consider model training costs
- Factor in data acquisition or labeling if needed

---

### Slide 15: Vision (Closing Slide)
**Goal:** Inspire the investor with the long-term potential

**Content:**
- Your 5-10 year vision
- How you'll change the industry
- The world you're building toward

**For AI Startups:**
- Connect to broader AI transformation themes
- Show category creation potential
- Inspirational but grounded

**Example:**
"We envision a world where [aspirational outcome]. By 2030, [Company Name] will be the AI mediation layer powering [specific use case] for [X] companies globally, enabling [transformative impact]."

---

### Slide 16: Contact / Thank You
**Elements:**
- Thank you message
- Your name and email
- Company website
- LinkedIn (optional)
- "Questions?" invitation

## AI Startup-Specific Pitch Deck Tips

### Technical Depth Balance:
- **Too Technical:** Lose non-technical investors
- **Too Simple:** Lose credibility with technical investors
- **Sweet Spot:** Explain the "what" and "why" of your AI, not the "how"
  - ✅ "Our NLP model analyzes customer conversations to predict churn with 92% accuracy"
  - ❌ "We use a transformer architecture with 8 attention heads and BERT embeddings"

### Differentiation for AI Startups:
- **Data advantage:** Unique dataset, proprietary training data, or data flywheel
- **Model performance:** Superior accuracy, speed, or cost
- **User experience:** AI that "just works" without ML expertise
- **Infrastructure:** Novel architecture or deployment approach
- **Domain specificity:** AI tuned for specific vertical vs. general-purpose

### Address AI-Specific Concerns:
Proactively address in your appendix (slides 17-20):
- **Model accuracy and reliability:** Show benchmarks, error rates
- **Data privacy and security:** How you handle sensitive data
- **Compute costs:** Unit economics of inference
- **Moat:** What prevents OpenAI/Google from crushing you?
- **Bias and safety:** How you ensure ethical AI use

## Design Best Practices

### Visual Design:
- **Consistent brand colors:** Use 2-3 primary colors throughout
- **Professional fonts:** Sans-serif (Helvetica, Arial, Inter) for body, optional serif for headers
- **Minimal text:** Max 3-5 bullets per slide, large font (18pt+)
- **High-quality visuals:** Product screenshots, charts, icons (not stock photos)
- **White space:** Don't cram slides - air breathes credibility

### Data Visualization:
- Use charts for trends (line charts for growth)
- Use icons/graphics to show comparisons
- Annotate key datapoints
- Keep it simple - one key insight per chart

### Avoid:
- ❌ Walls of text
- ❌ Tiny fonts
- ❌ Generic stock photos
- ❌ Overly technical diagrams
- ❌ Too many slides (under 20 is ideal)

## Pre-Send Checklist

Before sending your deck via DocSend:

- ✅ Proofread for typos (get 2-3 people to review)
- ✅ Check all numbers for accuracy and consistency
- ✅ Remove any placeholder text or TODOs
- ✅ Ensure all visuals are high-resolution
- ✅ Verify links work (if you include website, etc.)
- ✅ Export as PDF (not PPT) for consistent viewing
- ✅ File size under 10MB
- ✅ Test on mobile - many investors view on phone
- ✅ Update date on cover slide to current month/year
- ✅ Add "Confidential" footer (optional)

## Tailoring for Different Audiences

### For Technical AI Investors (AI-focused funds, engineer-angels):
- Add more technical depth to solution slide
- Include model performance metrics
- Reference relevant research papers or techniques
- Show GitHub stars, API usage, or developer adoption

### For General Pre-Seed/Angel Investors:
- Focus on business metrics (traction, revenue, growth)
- Simplify AI explanation - lead with benefits
- Emphasize market size and GTM
- Show comparable successful AI exits

### For Industry-Specific Investors:
- Lead with domain problem (they'll get it immediately)
- Show industry expertise and relationships
- Reference industry-specific metrics/benchmarks
- Highlight regulatory or compliance advantages

## Pitch Deck Versions to Maintain

Create 3 versions:

1. **Teaser Deck (5-7 slides):**
   - Problem, Solution, Traction, Team, Ask
   - For cold outreach or quick intros
   - Send this first, then follow with full deck

2. **Main Deck (15-18 slides):**
   - Full deck as outlined above
   - For investors who've expressed interest
   - This goes in DocSend

3. **Presentation Deck (15-18 slides + appendix):**
   - Same as main deck but designed for live presentation
   - Add speaker notes
   - Include appendix for Q&A
   - For pitch meetings and demos
