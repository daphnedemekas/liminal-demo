```text
# Background Resources for Curiosity Discovery

This document defines concepts that can be referenced in prompts.
Format: [CONCEPT_NAME]: definition

## Curiosity Types

[I_TYPE_CURIOSITY]: Interest-type epistemic curiosity. Driven by the anticipated pleasure of learning something new that they find fascinating. These users want "fun facts" and "wonder." Maximally activated when discovering something completely new. They describe curiosity with positive affect: "I love not knowing," "I'm excited to learn about this," "That's so cool!" They're drawn to novelty and breadth.

[D_TYPE_CURIOSITY]: Deprivation-type epistemic curiosity. Driven by the "itch" of not knowing. A restless, almost uncomfortable feeling that a gap exists in their knowledge. Optimally stimulated when people lack specific pieces of information they wish to incorporate into an existing knowledge-set. They describe curiosity with tension: "It bugs me that I don't understand this," "I need to know why," "I can't stop thinking about this gap." They're drawn to depth and completion.

## Learning Frameworks

[ZPD]: Zone of Proximal Development. The area between what a learner can do without help and what they can do with help. Epistemic curiosity is maximally activated when people feel they are "on the verge of knowing or understanding." Content that's too easy produces boredom; too hard produces anxiety. The sweet spot is just beyond current capability but within reach.

[RPL]: Region of Proximal Learning. Items that are on the "tip of their tongue" - items they almost know but don't quite. The sweet spot where curiosity peaks. This is more specific than ZPD: it's about identifying the exact knowledge gap that feels solvable and satisfying to close. People can often articulate being "so close" to understanding something.

[INFORMATION_GAP_THEORY]: Loewenstein's theory that curiosity peaks when we have a small amount of information about a topic, but realize a specific piece is missing. Too little information = no curiosity (can't even formulate the question). Too much information = no curiosity (already know it). The gap must be perceivable and feel closeable.

## Interest Texture (RIASEC)

[INVESTIGATIVE]: Likes ideas and logic (The "Why"). Drawn to understanding mechanisms and principles. Uses language like "How does this work?" "What's the underlying principle?" "I want to understand the system." Values precision, models, and explanatory power. Often asks follow-up questions about causation.

[ARTISTIC]: Likes elegance and strangeness (The "Aesthetic"). Drawn to beauty and unusual perspectives. Uses language like "That's beautiful," "I love how weird that is," "There's something elegant about this." Values novelty, aesthetics, and creative connections. Often attracted to counterintuitive or surprising ideas.

[REALISTIC_SOCIAL]: Likes people and problems (The "Utility"). Drawn to practical applications and impact. Uses language like "How would I use this?" "Who does this help?" "What problem does this solve?" Values applicability, tangible outcomes, and real-world relevance. Often asks about examples and use cases.

## Interest Development Phases

[TRIGGERED_SITUATIONAL]: Initial spark of interest from external stimulus. Fleeting, easily extinguished. User is responding to something they just encountered - a conversation, an article, a question someone asked them. They may not have thought about this topic before. Characterized by tentative language: "I was just wondering about..." "Someone mentioned this and..." "I saw something about..."

[MAINTAINED_SITUATIONAL]: Interest persists beyond initial trigger. Re-engagement occurs. User has returned to the topic multiple times or can describe thinking about it over days/weeks. They've sought out additional content. Characterized by: "I keep coming back to this," "I've watched a few videos about this," "I've been thinking about this for a while."

[EMERGING_INDIVIDUAL]: Personal ownership develops. User generates their own questions. User has moved beyond consuming content to formulating their own questions and pursuing specific angles. They have preferences about what aspect interests them most. Characterized by: "What I really want to know is..." "My specific question is..." "I'm curious about the [specific aspect] rather than [other aspect]."

[WELL_DEVELOPED_INDIVIDUAL]: Deep, self-directed exploration. Existing knowledge needs complication, not introduction. User has substantial prior knowledge and is seeking to refine, challenge, or extend it. They can articulate what they already know and what remains unclear. Characterized by: "I understand X, but I'm confused about Y," "I have a model of this, but it breaks down when..." "I know the basics, I want to go deeper on..."

## Confusion Types

[PRODUCTIVE_CONFUSION]: Confronted with information inconsistent with existing knowledge, encounter an impasse, but eventually find a way to resolve it. Generative and motivating. This is the "good struggle" - the learner feels challenged but capable. Signs: asking clarifying questions, trying to reconcile inconsistencies, building new mental models. This drives deep learning.

[HOPELESS_CONFUSION]: Unable to resolve the impasse, resulting in frustration and boredom. Demotivating. The learner feels lost and doesn't see a path forward. Signs: shutting down, changing topics abruptly, expressing inadequacy ("I'm just not smart enough for this"), vague questions. This kills curiosity.

## Motivation (Self-Determination Theory)

[AUTONOMY]: Feeling of choice and control over learning direction. "I get to decide what to explore." When violated, learning feels imposed or coercive. Support autonomy by offering choices, asking what they want to focus on, respecting their "no" when they want to move on. Undermined by: "You should learn this," "This is important," "Let me tell you about..."

[COMPETENCE]: Feeling capable of understanding, having existing knowledge validated. "I can do this." When violated, learning feels overwhelming or infantilizing. Support competence by acknowledging what they already know, scaffolding appropriately, celebrating insight. Undermined by: too-hard content with no support, too-easy content that's patronizing, ignoring their existing knowledge.

[RELATEDNESS]: Connection to others' values, identity, or social relevance. "This matters to people I care about" or "This connects to who I am." When violated, learning feels isolated or irrelevant. Support relatedness by connecting to their goals, their community, their identity. Undermined by: purely abstract content with no personal connection, dismissing their reasons for caring.

## Value Types (Expectancy-Value Theory)

[INTRINSIC_VALUE]: Interest for its own sake. "I just want to know." The topic is inherently enjoyable or satisfying. This is the purest form of curiosity - no external motivation needed. Language: "I find this fascinating," "I love thinking about this," "It's just cool."

[UTILITY_VALUE]: Usefulness for goals. "This will help me at work / with my project / in my life." The topic is instrumentally valuable. Not inherently interesting, but serves a purpose. Language: "I need to understand this for..." "This would help me with..." "I could use this to..."

[IDENTITY_VALUE]: Connection to who they want to be. "I want to be the kind of person who understands this." The topic is tied to self-concept or aspirational identity. Language: "I want to be someone who..." "I should know this because..." "People like me understand..."

[COST]: Effort, time, or emotional investment required. High cost reduces engagement. This is the friction that competes with value. Types of cost: time ("This will take forever"), effort ("This is too hard"), emotional ("This makes me feel dumb"), opportunity cost ("I could be learning something else"). Reduce cost by: breaking into smaller pieces, scaffolding, emotional support, showing tractability.

## Question Strategies

[FRICTION_PROBE]: "Where does your understanding stop feeling crisp?" - Identifies knowledge edge. Purpose: Locate the exact boundary between what they know confidently and what becomes fuzzy. More effective than asking "What don't you understand?" because it normalizes partial understanding. Follow-up: "Walk me through your explanation until you hit that point."

[COMPRESSION_TEST]: "Give me your best 20-second explanation, even if it's wrong." - Reveals current model and gaps. Purpose: Elicit their existing mental model in compressed form. The compression forces them to reveal what they think is central. "Even if it's wrong" gives permission to try. Follow-up: "What part of that explanation feels shakiest to you?"

[BOUNDARY_QUESTION]: "What would you need to know to feel like you actually understood this?" - Gets at implicit success criterion. Purpose: Uncover their definition of "understanding" for this specific topic. Different people have different bars. Some want mechanism, some want application, some want intuition. Follow-up: "If you woke up tomorrow understanding this, what could you do that you can't do now?"

[COUNTERFACTUAL_HOOK]: "If you really understood this, what would change?" - Tests utility value. Purpose: Clarify stakes and value. Helps user articulate why this matters (or discover it doesn't). If they can't answer, might indicate low value. Follow-up: "Is that change important to you?"

[IDENTITY_PROBE]: "Is this connected to something you're trying to become?" - Tests attainment value. Purpose: Uncover identity-based motivation. Some curiosity is aspirational - about becoming a certain kind of person. If present, this is powerful fuel. Follow-up: "What kind of person are you trying to be?"

[DEFLECTION_RESPONSE]: When user says "I don't know" or deflects. Purpose: Recognize defensive response to uncertainty. Don't push harder - reduce cost and stakes. Strategies: "That's totally fine - we can explore something else," "What if we came at this from a different angle?" "Is this actually interesting to you, or should we pivot?"

[VALUE_TEST]: When unsure if topic has genuine value. Purpose: Check if topic is worth pursuing or if it's superficial. Ask: "Why does this matter to you?" or "What drew you to this in the first place?" If they can't articulate value, might be triggered situational interest only - tread lightly.

[SCOPE_REDUCTION]: When topic is too broad. Purpose: Narrow to tractable 5-minute learning target. Ask: "If we could make just one thing click in the next 5 minutes, what would be most satisfying?" or "What's the smallest piece of this that would feel like progress?"
```

```python
def _schema_dump_for_llm(self, schema: DiscoverySchema) -> Dict[str, Any]:
        """
        Reduce schema payload size for LLM prompts.

        The controller/readiness prompts don't need the full unbounded schema;
        sending only the most salient items saves tokens and latency.

        Phase is auto-detected from schema.interview_state.goal_identified:
        - goal_discovery: Include goal_candidates, no teaching_candidates
        - teaching_discovery: Include teaching_candidates, expose user_goal
        """
        try:
            # Determine phase from goal_identified
            phase = "teaching_discovery" if schema.interview_state.goal_identified else "goal_discovery"

            top_themes = sorted(
                schema.conversational_themes,
                key=lambda t: t.readiness_score,
                reverse=True
            )[:12]

            max_turns = max(4, get_conversation_max_turns(default=8))
            turns_elapsed = schema.interview_state.turns_elapsed
            urgency = min(1.0, max(0.0, turns_elapsed / float(max_turns - 1)))
            has_concrete_theme = any(t.theme_type == "concrete_topic" for t in schema.conversational_themes)
            if not has_concrete_theme and turns_elapsed >= 2:
                urgency = min(1.0, urgency + 0.2)

            # Base schema
            result = {
                "session_id": schema.session_id,
                "derived": {
                    "turns_elapsed": turns_elapsed,
                    "max_turns": max_turns,
                    "urgency": urgency,
                    "phase": phase,
                },
                "user_profile": schema.user_profile.model_dump(),
                "interview_state": schema.interview_state.model_dump(),
                "conversational_themes": [t.model_dump() for t in top_themes],
                "controller": schema.controller.model_dump() if schema.controller else None,
                "teaching_recommendation": schema.teaching_recommendation.model_dump() if schema.teaching_recommendation else None,
            }

            # Phase-specific candidate inclusion
            if phase == "goal_discovery":
                # Goal Discovery: Include goal_candidates, minimal teaching_candidates
                top_goals = sorted(
                    schema.goal_candidates,
                    key=lambda c: c.readiness_score,
                    reverse=True
                )[:8]
                result["goal_candidates"] = [g.model_dump() for g in top_goals]
                result["teaching_candidates"] = []  # Not relevant in Phase 1
            else:
                # Teaching Discovery: Include teaching_candidates, reference user_goal
                top_teaching = sorted(
                    schema.teaching_candidates,
                    key=lambda c: c.readiness_score,
                    reverse=True
                )[:8]
                result["teaching_candidates"] = [t.model_dump() for t in top_teaching]
                result["goal_candidates"] = []  # Goal already identified
                # Ensure user_goal is prominently available
                result["user_goal"] = schema.interview_state.user_goal

            return result
        except Exception:
            # Worst case, fall back to full dump.
            return schema.model_dump()
```

```text
WHAT IS A GOAL CANDIDATE?

A goal candidate is a concrete learning outcome that:
1. Would take MULTIPLE LESSONS to accomplish (not a single 15-minute explanation)
2. Is outcome-oriented ("be able to X", "understand Y intuitively", "build Z")
3. Is break-apartable into smaller teachable sub-goals
4. Represents what the user actually wants to achieve

EXAMPLES OF VALID GOAL CANDIDATES:

✅ "Become conversational in Spanish"
   - Outcome: able to have basic conversations
   - Multi-lesson: grammar, vocabulary, listening, speaking practice
   - Break-apart: verb conjugations, common phrases, pronunciation, etc.

✅ "Understand quantum mechanics intuitively"
   - Outcome: intuitive grasp of core concepts
   - Multi-lesson: superposition, measurement, entanglement, wave functions
   - Break-apart: each concept is a teaching target

✅ "Build a full-stack web application"
   - Outcome: working deployed app
   - Multi-lesson: frontend, backend, database, deployment
   - Break-apart: React components, API design, SQL queries, etc.

✅ "Develop geometric intuition for linear algebra"
   - Outcome: visual/spatial understanding
   - Multi-lesson: vectors, matrices, transformations, eigenvalues
   - Break-apart: each geometric concept is teachable

✅ "Master chess endgames"
   - Outcome: competence in endgame positions
   - Multi-lesson: king+rook, king+queen, pawn endgames, opposition
   - Break-apart: each endgame type is a lesson

✅ "Understand how neural networks work"
   - Outcome: mental model of neural network functioning
   - Multi-lesson: architecture, forward pass, backprop, training, optimization
   - Break-apart: each component is teachable

EXAMPLES OF INVALID GOAL CANDIDATES:

❌ "How does backpropagation work?"
   - This is a TEACHING CANDIDATE (single lesson, specific mechanism)
   - Not a multi-lesson outcome

❌ "Learn programming"
   - Too vague, not concrete enough
   - Need more specificity: programming what? for what purpose?

❌ "Understand everything about physics"
   - Too broad, not break-apartable into achievable scope
   - Need to narrow to specific domain

❌ "Be less confused"
   - Abstract feeling, not a concrete learning outcome
   - This is a conversational theme, not a goal
```

```python
        """

        goal = self.schema.interview_state.proposed_goal
        goal_id = self.schema.interview_state.proposed_goal_id

        # Track this goal as accepted so we don't re-propose it
        if goal_id is not None:
            self.schema.interview_state.accepted_goal_ids.append(goal_id)
        
        # Record turn number for cooldown (don't propose another goal immediately)
        self.schema.interview_state.last_goal_accepted_turn = self.schema.interview_state.turns_elapsed

        # Clear proposal but stay in Phase 1 (exploration continues)
        self.schema.interview_state.proposed_goal = None
        self.schema.interview_state.proposed_goal_id = None
        # goal_identified stays False - can discover more goals

        print(f"\n[Orchestrator] ===== GOAL ACCEPTED =====")
        print(f"[Orchestrator] Goal: '{goal}'")
        print(f"[Orchestrator] Exploration continues - frontend creates goal panel")

        # Add acceptance to conversation history
        confirmation_message = f"Great! I'll help you explore **{goal}** in a new panel. You can continue exploring other interests here."
        self.conversation_history.append({
            "role": "user",
            "content": "[Accepted goal]"
        })
        self.conversation_history.append({
            "role": "assistant",
            "content": confirmation_message
        })

        # Save the updated state AND conversation history
        self.db.save_session_state(self.session_id, self.schema.model_dump())
```

### Understanding Markers (18 total)

The system tracks 18 understanding markers across four categories:

**Cognitive Markers (basic understanding):**
- `recall` - Can reproduce key facts, terms, and procedures
- `explanation` - Can explain concepts in own words
- `application` - Can use knowledge in familiar situations
- `analysis` - Can break down and examine relationships between parts

**Transfer Markers (deeper understanding):**
- `transfer` - Can apply concepts to novel domains or contexts
- `analogy_generation` - Can create relevant analogies to explain concepts
- `connection_making` - Links new knowledge to prior knowledge effectively
- `problem_decomposition` - Can break complex problems into manageable parts

**Metacognitive Markers (self-awareness):**
- `self_explanation` - Can articulate their thinking process
- `error_detection` - Can identify mistakes in reasoning (own or others')
- `metacognitive_awareness` - Knows what they know and don't know
- `confidence_calibration` - Confidence level matches actual understanding
- `limitation_awareness` - Knows boundaries and edge cases of applicability

**Reasoning Markers (higher-order thinking):**
- `prediction` - Can anticipate outcomes based on understanding
- `counterfactual_reasoning` - Can reason about alternatives and 'what if' scenarios
- `question_generation` - Asks meaningful, probing questions

**Communication Markers (demonstrating mastery):**
- `teaching_ability` - Can explain concepts clearly to others
- `intuitive_grasp` - Has gut-level, automatic understanding

Each marker has three levels:
- `NOT_YET` - No evidence of this type of understanding
- `DEVELOPING` - Some evidence, but incomplete or inconsistent
- `STRONG` - Clear, consistent evidence of this understanding

```text
   - Don't just lecture - include something for them to react to
   - End with question or invitation about 70% of the time

3. NO QUIZ ATMOSPHERE
   - Questions should feel collaborative, not testing
   - "I'm curious what you think about..." not "Can you tell me what X is?"
   - It's okay if they don't know - that's what we're here for

4. SHOW GENUINE INTEREST
   - React to their good questions/insights
   - Build on what they say (not just acknowledge)
   - Share interesting connections when relevant

5. ADAPTIVE PACING
   - If they're confused: slow down, use simpler language, more examples
   - If they're engaged: match their energy, go deeper
   - If they're bored: pick up pace, add challenge

6. NO EXCESSIVE PRAISE
   - Skip "Great question!" and "Excellent point!" fillers
   - Substantive acknowledgment is fine: "That's exactly the key insight..."
   - Focus on content, not evaluation

7. PARAGRAPH BREAKS
   - Use blank lines between paragraphs for readability
   - Don't wall-of-text them

FORMAT:
- 2-3 paragraphs typical
- Use markdown for emphasis (**bold** for key terms)
- Blank line between paragraphs
- End with something to respond to (question, prediction to verify, concept to explore)

OUTPUT: Return ONLY your response text. No JSON, no meta-commentary.
```

```python
@app.websocket("/ws/teaching/{session_id}")
async def teaching_websocket(websocket: WebSocket, session_id: str):
    """WebSocket endpoint for teaching curriculum conversation."""
    await websocket.accept()
    
    # Get orchestrator from memory
    orchestrator = teaching_sessions.get(session_id)
    
    if not orchestrator:
        await websocket.send_json({
            "type": "error",
            "message": "Teaching session expired. Please restart."
        })
        await websocket.close()
        return
    
    try:
        while True:
            # Receive message from frontend
            data = await websocket.receive_json()
            user_message = data.get("content", "")
            
            if not user_message:
                continue
            
            # Send status
            await websocket.send_json({
                "type": "status",
                "status": "Processing..."
            })
            
            # Process message through teaching orchestrator (in thread pool)
            import asyncio
            loop = asyncio.get_event_loop()
            response = await loop.run_in_executor(
                None,
                orchestrator.process_user_message,
                user_message
            )
            
            # Get current state for markers/progress
            schema = orchestrator.get_schema()
            
            # Check if teaching phase is complete
            if orchestrator.is_complete():
                await websocket.send_json({
                    "type": "teaching_complete",
                    "content": response,
                    "message": "You've demonstrated strong understanding of this topic!",
                    "final_markers": schema.get("understanding_markers", [])
                })
            else:
                # Send regular teaching message with state updates
                await websocket.send_json({
                    "type": "teaching_message",
                    "content": response,
                    "curriculum_progress": {
                        "current_step": schema.get("current_step_index", 0),
                        "total_steps": len(schema.get("curriculum_plan", {}).get("steps", [])),
                        "completed_steps": len(schema.get("curriculum_plan", {}).get("completed_step_ids", []))
                    },
                    "narrative_summary": schema.get("narrative_summary", ""),
                    "understanding_markers": [
                        {
                            "id": m.get("id"),
                            "name": m.get("name"),
                            "level": m.get("level"),
                            "evidence": m.get("evidence", [])[-1] if m.get("evidence") else None
                        }
                        for m in schema.get("understanding_markers", [])
                        if m.get("level") != "not_yet"  # Only send markers with evidence
                    ]
                })
                
    except WebSocketDisconnect:
        print(f"[Teaching WS] Disconnected: {session_id[:8]}...")
```

```python
    try:
        conv_session = session.query(ConversationSession).filter_by(
            goal_id=goal_id,
            teaching_candidate_id=teaching_candidate_id,
            session_type='teaching'
        ).order_by(ConversationSession.started_at.desc()).first()
        
        if conv_session:
            return {
                "session_id": conv_session.session_id,
                "conversation_history": conv_session.conversation_history or [],
                "schema_state": conv_session.schema_state,
                "turns_elapsed": conv_session.turns_elapsed,
                "goal_id": conv_session.goal_id,
                "teaching_candidate_id": conv_session.teaching_candidate_id
            }
        return None
    finally:
        session.close()
```
