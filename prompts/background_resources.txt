# Background Resources for Curiosity Discovery

This document defines concepts that can be referenced in prompts.
Format: [CONCEPT_NAME]: definition

## Curiosity Types

[I_TYPE_CURIOSITY]: Interest-type epistemic curiosity. Driven by the anticipated pleasure of learning something new that they find fascinating. These users want "fun facts" and "wonder." Maximally activated when discovering something completely new. They describe curiosity with positive affect: "I love not knowing," "I'm excited to learn about this," "That's so cool!" They're drawn to novelty and breadth.

[D_TYPE_CURIOSITY]: Deprivation-type epistemic curiosity. Driven by the "itch" of not knowing. A restless, almost uncomfortable feeling that a gap exists in their knowledge. Optimally stimulated when people lack specific pieces of information they wish to incorporate into an existing knowledge-set. They describe curiosity with tension: "It bugs me that I don't understand this," "I need to know why," "I can't stop thinking about this gap." They're drawn to depth and completion.

## Learning Frameworks

[ZPD]: Zone of Proximal Development. The area between what a learner can do without help and what they can do with help. Epistemic curiosity is maximally activated when people feel they are "on the verge of knowing or understanding." Content that's too easy produces boredom; too hard produces anxiety. The sweet spot is just beyond current capability but within reach.

[RPL]: Region of Proximal Learning. Items that are on the "tip of their tongue" - items they almost know but don't quite. The sweet spot where curiosity peaks. This is more specific than ZPD: it's about identifying the exact knowledge gap that feels solvable and satisfying to close. People can often articulate being "so close" to understanding something.

[INFORMATION_GAP_THEORY]: Loewenstein's theory that curiosity peaks when we have a small amount of information about a topic, but realize a specific piece is missing. Too little information = no curiosity (can't even formulate the question). Too much information = no curiosity (already know it). The gap must be perceivable and feel closeable.

## Interest Texture (RIASEC)

[INVESTIGATIVE]: Likes ideas and logic (The "Why"). Drawn to understanding mechanisms and principles. Uses language like "How does this work?" "What's the underlying principle?" "I want to understand the system." Values precision, models, and explanatory power. Often asks follow-up questions about causation.

[ARTISTIC]: Likes elegance and strangeness (The "Aesthetic"). Drawn to beauty and unusual perspectives. Uses language like "That's beautiful," "I love how weird that is," "There's something elegant about this." Values novelty, aesthetics, and creative connections. Often attracted to counterintuitive or surprising ideas.

[REALISTIC_SOCIAL]: Likes people and problems (The "Utility"). Drawn to practical applications and impact. Uses language like "How would I use this?" "Who does this help?" "What problem does this solve?" Values applicability, tangible outcomes, and real-world relevance. Often asks about examples and use cases.

## Interest Development Phases

[TRIGGERED_SITUATIONAL]: Initial spark of interest from external stimulus. Fleeting, easily extinguished. User is responding to something they just encountered - a conversation, an article, a question someone asked them. They may not have thought about this topic before. Characterized by tentative language: "I was just wondering about..." "Someone mentioned this and..." "I saw something about..."

[MAINTAINED_SITUATIONAL]: Interest persists beyond initial trigger. Re-engagement occurs. User has returned to the topic multiple times or can describe thinking about it over days/weeks. They've sought out additional content. Characterized by: "I keep coming back to this," "I've watched a few videos about this," "I've been thinking about this for a while."

[EMERGING_INDIVIDUAL]: Personal ownership develops. User generates their own questions. User has moved beyond consuming content to formulating their own questions and pursuing specific angles. They have preferences about what aspect interests them most. Characterized by: "What I really want to know is..." "My specific question is..." "I'm curious about the [specific aspect] rather than [other aspect]."

[WELL_DEVELOPED_INDIVIDUAL]: Deep, self-directed exploration. Existing knowledge needs complication, not introduction. User has substantial prior knowledge and is seeking to refine, challenge, or extend it. They can articulate what they already know and what remains unclear. Characterized by: "I understand X, but I'm confused about Y," "I have a model of this, but it breaks down when..." "I know the basics, I want to go deeper on..."

## Confusion Types

[PRODUCTIVE_CONFUSION]: Confronted with information inconsistent with existing knowledge, encounter an impasse, but eventually find a way to resolve it. Generative and motivating. This is the "good struggle" - the learner feels challenged but capable. Signs: asking clarifying questions, trying to reconcile inconsistencies, building new mental models. This drives deep learning.

[HOPELESS_CONFUSION]: Unable to resolve the impasse, resulting in frustration and boredom. Demotivating. The learner feels lost and doesn't see a path forward. Signs: shutting down, changing topics abruptly, expressing inadequacy ("I'm just not smart enough for this"), vague questions. This kills curiosity.

## Motivation (Self-Determination Theory)

[AUTONOMY]: Feeling of choice and control over learning direction. "I get to decide what to explore." When violated, learning feels imposed or coercive. Support autonomy by offering choices, asking what they want to focus on, respecting their "no" when they want to move on. Undermined by: "You should learn this," "This is important," "Let me tell you about..."

[COMPETENCE]: Feeling capable of understanding, having existing knowledge validated. "I can do this." When violated, learning feels overwhelming or infantilizing. Support competence by acknowledging what they already know, scaffolding appropriately, celebrating insight. Undermined by: too-hard content with no support, too-easy content that's patronizing, ignoring their existing knowledge.

[RELATEDNESS]: Connection to others' values, identity, or social relevance. "This matters to people I care about" or "This connects to who I am." When violated, learning feels isolated or irrelevant. Support relatedness by connecting to their goals, their community, their identity. Undermined by: purely abstract content with no personal connection, dismissing their reasons for caring.

## Value Types (Expectancy-Value Theory)

[INTRINSIC_VALUE]: Interest for its own sake. "I just want to know." The topic is inherently enjoyable or satisfying. This is the purest form of curiosity - no external motivation needed. Language: "I find this fascinating," "I love thinking about this," "It's just cool."

[UTILITY_VALUE]: Usefulness for goals. "This will help me at work / with my project / in my life." The topic is instrumentally valuable. Not inherently interesting, but serves a purpose. Language: "I need to understand this for..." "This would help me with..." "I could use this to..."

[IDENTITY_VALUE]: Connection to who they want to be. "I want to be the kind of person who understands this." The topic is tied to self-concept or aspirational identity. Language: "I want to be someone who..." "I should know this because..." "People like me understand..."

[COST]: Effort, time, or emotional investment required. High cost reduces engagement. This is the friction that competes with value. Types of cost: time ("This will take forever"), effort ("This is too hard"), emotional ("This makes me feel dumb"), opportunity cost ("I could be learning something else"). Reduce cost by: breaking into smaller pieces, scaffolding, emotional support, showing tractability.

## Question Strategies

[FRICTION_PROBE]: "Where does your understanding stop feeling crisp?" - Identifies knowledge edge. Purpose: Locate the exact boundary between what they know confidently and what becomes fuzzy. More effective than asking "What don't you understand?" because it normalizes partial understanding. Follow-up: "Walk me through your explanation until you hit that point."

[COMPRESSION_TEST]: "Give me your best 20-second explanation, even if it's wrong." - Reveals current model and gaps. Purpose: Elicit their existing mental model in compressed form. The compression forces them to reveal what they think is central. "Even if it's wrong" gives permission to try. Follow-up: "What part of that explanation feels shakiest to you?"

[BOUNDARY_QUESTION]: "What would you need to know to feel like you actually understood this?" - Gets at implicit success criterion. Purpose: Uncover their definition of "understanding" for this specific topic. Different people have different bars. Some want mechanism, some want application, some want intuition. Follow-up: "If you woke up tomorrow understanding this, what could you do that you can't do now?"

[COUNTERFACTUAL_HOOK]: "If you really understood this, what would change?" - Tests utility value. Purpose: Clarify stakes and value. Helps user articulate why this matters (or discover it doesn't). If they can't answer, might indicate low value. Follow-up: "Is that change important to you?"

[IDENTITY_PROBE]: "Is this connected to something you're trying to become?" - Tests attainment value. Purpose: Uncover identity-based motivation. Some curiosity is aspirational - about becoming a certain kind of person. If present, this is powerful fuel. Follow-up: "What kind of person are you trying to be?"

[DEFLECTION_RESPONSE]: When user says "I don't know" or deflects. Purpose: Recognize defensive response to uncertainty. Don't push harder - reduce cost and stakes. Strategies: "That's totally fine - we can explore something else," "What if we came at this from a different angle?" "Is this actually interesting to you, or should we pivot?"

[VALUE_TEST]: When unsure if topic has genuine value. Purpose: Check if topic is worth pursuing or if it's superficial. Ask: "Why does this matter to you?" or "What drew you to this in the first place?" If they can't articulate value, might be triggered situational interest only - tread lightly.

[SCOPE_REDUCTION]: When topic is too broad. Purpose: Narrow to tractable 5-minute learning target. Ask: "If we could make just one thing click in the next 5 minutes, what would be most satisfying?" or "What's the smallest piece of this that would feel like progress?"

## Teaching Angle Selection (RIASEC-based)

When generating teaching content, select the primary teaching angle based on the user's RIASEC profile:

[INVESTIGATIVE_TEACHING]: Start with mechanisms and principles. 'Here's HOW it works...' Lead with the system, the math, the logic. Good for users who ask 'why?' and 'how?'

[ARTISTIC_TEACHING]: Start with intuition and visualization. 'Picture this...' Lead with analogies, visual metaphors, elegant insights. Good for users who respond to beauty and surprise.

[REALISTIC_SOCIAL_TEACHING]: Start with applications and impact. 'Here's what this lets you DO...' Lead with use cases, practical examples, real-world impact. Good for users who ask 'so what?' and 'how do I use this?'

Default to INVESTIGATIVE_TEACHING if RIASEC profile is not yet determined.
