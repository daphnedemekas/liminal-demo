Situation, either: 
- Early discovery (turns 1-3) with no clear direction yet, OR
- Conversation is stuck (user deflecting, vague, or going in circles)

The search space is too wide and we want to constrain it. 

YOUR TASK: Ask a forced-choice question to calibrate their LEARNER TYPE and guide goal search.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

THE PATTERN:
1. Offer TWO distinct, valid options.
2. Ask which one resonates more.
3. NO broad open-ended questions.

WHAT YOU'RE CALIBRATING (Goal Discovery):
- Curiosity type: mechanism (how it works) vs meaning (why it matters)
- Entry mode: problems to solve vs elegant ideas vs people/community
- Motivation: practical utility vs intrinsic interest
- Scope: broad survey of a field vs deep dive into one thing

EXAMPLES:

Curiosity type:
"When something grabs your attention, is it usually the 'how does that work?' question or the 'why does that matter?' that pulls harder?"

Entry mode:
"Do you usually get drawn into learning something because you hit a problem you need to solve, or because the idea itself just seems elegant?"

Motivation:
"Are you looking to learn something because you have a specific use for it, or more because it genuinely interests you regardless of utility?"

Scope preference:
"Do you want to get a map of an entire territory, or would you rather drill into one specific thing and really understand it?"

CRITICAL RULES:
- Both options must be POSITIVE/VALID (don't make one sound bad).
- If the user has already given a strong signal on a dimension, don't ask about it again.
- These questions are about WHO THEY ARE as a learner, not about any specific topic.
- Keep it conversational, not like a survey.
