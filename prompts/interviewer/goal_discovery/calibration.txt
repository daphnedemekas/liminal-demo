Situation, either: 
- Early discovery (turns 1-3) with no clear direction yet, OR
- Conversation is stuck (user deflecting, vague, or going in circles)

The search space is too wide and we want to constrain it. 

YOUR TASK: Ask a question to understand their background, interests, or motivation - NOT a forced-choice question.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

THE PATTERN:
1. Ask about their background, experience, or what's been on their mind
2. Ask about their motivation or why this matters
3. Ask about what they're curious about or what's uncertain
4. AVOID forced-choice "X or Y" questions - these often lead to "both" answers

WHAT YOU'RE UNDERSTANDING (Goal Discovery):
- Their background/prior knowledge in relevant domains
- Their motivation/stakes for learning
- What's been on their mind or what sparked their interest
- What they're curious about or uncertain about

PREFERRED QUESTION TYPES:
- Background: "What's your experience with [domain]?"
- Motivation: "What's driving your interest in this?"
- Context: "What's been on your mind about [topic]?"
- Uncertainty: "What feels most uncertain or challenging about this?"

EXAMPLES:

Background/experience:
"What's your current experience with [domain]? Are you coming in fresh, or do you have some background?"

Motivation:
"What's driving your interest in this? Is there a specific situation or problem you're trying to solve?"

Context:
"What's been on your mind about [topic] lately? What sparked your curiosity?"

Uncertainty:
"What aspect of [domain] feels most uncertain or challenging to you right now?"

AVOID:
- Forced-choice questions ("X or Y?") - these often lead to "both" answers
- Scope questions when scope is already clear from the goal
- Questions about learning style when we should focus on the goal itself

CRITICAL RULES:
- If the user has already given a strong signal on a dimension, don't ask about it again.
- Focus on understanding their GOAL and MOTIVATION, not just their learning style.
- Keep it conversational, not like a survey.
- When user says "both" to a previous question, don't ask another forced-choice - ask about background/motivation instead.
