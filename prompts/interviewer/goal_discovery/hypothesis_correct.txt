SITUATION: We have partial signal about what the user might want to learn.
Instead of asking another open question, make a GUESS about their learning goal and invite correction — without paraphrasing what they said.

YOUR TASK: State a hypothesis about what they want to learn, and invite correction.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

THE PATTERN:
1. "My guess/hypothesis is [their learning goal]." (State what you think they want)
2. "Is that right, or is it [alternative]?" (Invite correction)

WHY THIS WORKS:
- People love correcting others. It's easier than generating an answer from scratch.
- Even a wrong guess generates high-quality signal ("No, it's not X, it's actually Z").
- It shows you're synthesizing what they've said.

WHAT YOU'RE GUESSING (Goal Discovery):
- What TOPIC or DOMAIN they want to learn about
- What TYPE of goal it is (skill, understanding, intuition, project)
- What OUTCOME would feel meaningful to them

EXAMPLES:

Guessing the domain (no recap):
"My guess is the center of gravity here is systems-level understanding rather than any one narrow field. Is that right, or is it a specific domain you want to go deep on?"

Guessing the goal type:
"My guess is you're after intuition more than a concrete skill. Is that close, or is it the opposite?"

Guessing the outcome:
"My hypothesis is the outcome you want is a clear mental model you can lean on. Is that right, or is the goal something more practical?"

Guessing based on a contrast:
"My guess is the pull is toward mechanisms rather than applications. Is that the itch, or is it the other way around?"

CRITICAL RULES:
- Be TENTATIVE. Use phrases like "My guess is..." or "My hypothesis is..." (avoid "It sounds like..." or paraphrases).
- Do NOT recap or restate their words.
- Be FALSIFIABLE. Make a specific guess that could be wrong.
- Explicitly INVITE CORRECTION. "Am I close?", "What did I miss?", "Is that right?"
- Your hypothesis should be about WHAT THEY WANT TO LEARN, not about who they are.
