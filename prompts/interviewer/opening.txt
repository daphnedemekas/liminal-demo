# Opening Question Bank

# Use "## Q:" prefix to mark actual questions (parser looks for this)
# Everything else is ignored (comments, headers, notes)

## Simple Direct Questions (No Frameworks Yet):

## Q: Right now, are you more drawn to filling in gaps in something you already care about, or exploring completely new territory?

## Q: When you want to understand something, which pull is usually stronger - how it works, or what it means?

## Q: Is your curiosity more like a spotlight on one thing, or more like a floodlight across many things?

## Q: Do you usually learn by toggling between concrete examples and abstract ideas, or do you prefer to stay at one level?

## Q: When something is ambiguous or uncertain, do you need to resolve it quickly, or are you comfortable sitting with open questions?

## Q: Do you prefer to start with details and build up, or start with the big picture and zoom in?

## Q: When you're learning something new, do you tend to follow tangents, or stay focused and finish what you started?

## To differentiate between I-type and D-type curiosity:

## Q: When you encounter something you don't understand, does it feel more like an invitation or an itch?

## Q: Do you tend to enjoy the process of figuring things out, or do you mostly want the answer?

## Q: When you learn something new, what's the better feeling - 'oh, that's fascinating' or 'ah, now it makes sense'?

## Q: If you had to choose: would you rather learn something surprising, or fill in a gap you've been aware of?

## Q: Do you want to explore a topic that is new to you, or get clearer on something you've already visited?

## Project-focused questions:
## Q: What's a project you've been thinking about but haven't started yet?
## Q: Is there something you're working on that you'd like to make more progress on?
## Q: What's a goal you keep coming back to but haven't fully figured out?

## To detect entry mode (people/problems/ideas):

## Q: Do you tend to get interested in things through people, problems, or ideas first?

## Q: Are you more likely to get pulled into something because someone you respect cares about it, because it could solve a real problem, or because the idea itself is elegant?

## Q: What's more likely to make you remember something - a vivid example, a clean principle, or knowing who discovered it?

## Epistemic style:

## Q: When you're curious about something, do you want to 'zoom in' on the tiny details or 'zoom out' to see how it connects to everything else?

## To detect uncertainty tolerance:

## Q: Do you prefer learning things that have clear answers or things where you have to hold ambiguity?

## Q: When you're partway through understanding something, does the incompleteness bother you or intrigue you?

## Q: Would you rather understand one thing really well, or have a rough map of many things?

## To detect motivation quality (intrinsic vs. external):

## Q: What's the difference for you between 'I should be interested in this' and truly being interested?

## Q: Is there something you feel like you ought to understand but haven't gotten around to? What's been stopping you?

## Q: Are you usually drawn to the elegance of a system, or the impact it has on people's lives?

## Curiosity type patterns:

## Q: Does your curiosity tend to be steady on a particular topic, or come and go in waves?

## Q: When learning a new topic, do you tend to follow rabbit holes, or finish what you started?

## Q: When something stops being interesting to you, what usually happened?
