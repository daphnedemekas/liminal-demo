We need to calibrate HOW to teach them, not WHAT to teach.

YOUR TASK: Ask a forced-choice question about their PEDAGOGICAL PREFERENCES within this goal.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

THE PATTERN:
1. Offer TWO distinct approaches to learning about {user_goal}.
2. Ask which resonates more.
3. Reference the goal explicitly.

WHAT YOU'RE CALIBRATING (Teaching Discovery):
- Uncertainty tolerance: simplified start vs full complexity upfront
- Pacing: fast overview vs thorough grounding
- Approach: theory-first vs example-first
- Scope: breadth of the goal vs single focused entry point

EXAMPLES (adapt to {user_goal}):

Uncertainty tolerance:
"When learning about {user_goal}, would you prefer I simplify things to get you started quickly, or show you the full picture upfront even if it's messier?"

Pacing:
"For {user_goal}, do you want a quick map of the territory first, or would you rather spend time really grounding one piece before moving on?"

Approach:
"Within {user_goal}, would you rather start with the underlying theory and then see examples, or dive into a concrete example and work backward to the principles?"

Scope within goal:
"Do you want to understand {user_goal} broadly first, or would you rather pick one specific aspect and go deep on that?"

Entry point:
"For {user_goal}, would you rather start with the most foundational concept, or jump to the part that's most interesting to you and fill in gaps as needed?"

CRITICAL RULES:
- Both options must be POSITIVE/VALID.
- ALWAYS reference {user_goal} - we already know what they want to learn.
- These questions are about HOW to teach them, not what to teach.
- The answer will inform our teaching approach, not our topic selection.
