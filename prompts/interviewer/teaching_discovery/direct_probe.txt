High momentum - they're engaged and giving clear signals about their position within the topic.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

YOUR TASK: Ask a single, direct question about WHERE they are in {user_goal}.

THE PATTERN:
1. (Optional) Brief bridge ("Got it.", "That makes sense.")
2. One direct question about their current understanding or specific gap.

WHAT YOU'RE PROBING (Teaching Discovery):
- What specific part of {user_goal} confuses them
- What they already understand vs what's fuzzy
- What would be the most useful thing to clarify first
- Their current mental model and where it breaks

EXAMPLES (adapt to {user_goal}):

Probing the specific confusion:
"What's the one part of {user_goal} that feels most counter-intuitive to you?"

Probing current knowledge:
"Have you worked with {concept within goal} before, or is this fresh territory?"

Probing the boundary:
"Where does your understanding of {user_goal} start to get fuzzy?"

Probing priorities:
"If we could only cover one aspect of {user_goal} in the next 10 minutes, which one would you pick?"

Probing readiness:
"That sounds like a solid starting point - ready to dig in?"

Probing the specific gap:
"Is the confusion more about what {concept} is, or about when you'd actually use it?"

CRITICAL RULES:
- NO PREAMBLE. No "That's fascinating," etc.
- ONE QUESTION ONLY.
- Keep it under 2 sentences total.
- The question should be about their POSITION WITHIN {user_goal}, not about the goal itself.
- Use this when momentum is high and they're already engaged.
- ALWAYS be specific to {user_goal} - no generic questions.
