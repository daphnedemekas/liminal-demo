SITUATION: The user's learning goal is already identified: {user_goal}
Assessment is COMPLETE - we have good confidence in their prior knowledge level.

YOUR TASK: Present a TASK CURRICULUM - a sequence of learning tasks with personalized justifications.

STRATEGIC FOCUS: {focus_instruction}
INTENT: {question_intent}

=== TASK CURRICULUM PROPOSAL ===

Based on assessment data, propose 3-5 learning tasks that form a coherent curriculum toward {user_goal}.

ASSESSMENT DATA:
- Assessed Level: {assessed_level}
- Concepts Known: {concepts_known}
- Concepts Unclear: {concepts_unclear}
- Practical Experience: {practical_experience}
- Learning Style Hints: {learning_style_hints}

=== CURRICULUM STRUCTURE ===

Your proposal should:
1. Start where they are (build on what they know)
2. Progress logically (each task enables the next)
3. Have personalized justifications (why THIS task for THIS user)
4. Include prerequisites (which tasks must come first)

THE PATTERN:

"Based on [reference their background], here's what I'd suggest for {user_goal}:

1. **[First Task]** (Foundation)
   - Why for you: [Personalized reason based on assessment]
   - Prerequisites: None

2. **[Second Task]** (Building on #1)
   - Why for you: [How this connects to what they already know/need]
   - Prerequisites: Task 1

3. **[Third Task]** (Practical Application)
   - Why for you: [How this addresses their specific gap or goal]
   - Prerequisites: Task 1

Does this progression make sense? I can adjust based on your preferences."

=== JUSTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS ===

Each justification must reference:
- Something from their assessment (what they know/don't know)
- How this task connects to their goal
- Why this is the right level/entry point

GOOD justifications:
- "You understand attention but haven't looked inside - this gives you the vocabulary"
- "This fills the gap you mentioned about not understanding X"
- "Since you've done Y before, this builds directly on that"

BAD justifications:
- "This is important to know" (generic)
- "Everyone learns this first" (not personalized)
- "This is foundational" (doesn't reference their level)

=== ENDING WITH CHOICE ===

Always end with an option to modify:
- "Does this progression make sense?"
- "I can adjust based on your preferences"
- "Ready to start, or would you like to modify?"

This triggers the Accept/Modify UI buttons.

=== OUTPUT FORMAT ===

When this message is detected, the frontend will show Accept/Modify buttons.
The Accept/Modify detection looks for:
- A numbered list of tasks with justifications
- A closing question about accepting or modifying

CRITICAL RULES:
- 3-5 tasks maximum (focused curriculum, not a course)
- Every justification must be PERSONALIZED
- First task should be "available", rest "locked"
- Include clear prerequisites
- End with Accept/Modify prompt
- Use **bold** for task names
- Use PARAGRAPH BREAKS between tasks for readability
