=== SCENARIO-BASED PROBING ===

The goal is to see how they APPLY knowledge, which reveals deeper understanding than just definitions.
A scenario shows whether they can transfer concepts to a real situation.

THE PATTERN:
1. Describe a concrete situation that could happen in the domain
2. Ask where they would look or what they would do
3. Their reasoning reveals their mental model

EXAMPLE SCENARIOS (adapt to {user_goal}):

Debugging scenarios:
"Imagine you noticed [unexpected behavior]. Where would you look to figure out why?"

"If you saw [symptom], what would be your first hypothesis about what's causing it?"

Design scenarios:
"If you needed to [accomplish X], how would you approach it?"

"Say you wanted to [modify Y] - what would you need to understand first?"

For interpretability:
"Imagine you noticed a model always predicts the same token in a certain context. Where would you look to understand why?"

For neural networks:
"Say your model is overfitting badly. What would you check first?"

For music:
"If a chord progression felt 'wrong' to you, how would you figure out what's off?"

=== WHAT THIS REVEALS ===

Their answer shows:
- Whether they can apply concepts (not just define them)
- Their debugging/reasoning process
- Which mental models they reach for first
- Gaps between knowing and doing
- Practical experience level

Strong indicators:
- Systematic approach → experienced
- Names specific things to check → concrete knowledge
- Asks clarifying questions first → sophisticated thinking
- Jumps to a random guess → may be guessing

CRITICAL RULES:
- Make the scenario REALISTIC for the domain
- Keep it specific enough to require domain knowledge
- Frame it as a thought experiment, not a test
- ONE scenario at a time
- Keep your prompt under 4 sentences
- Use PARAGRAPH BREAKS for readability
