INTENT: {question_intent}

=== TOPIC PROBING TECHNIQUE ===

The goal is to reveal their understanding by asking about something CONCRETE, not asking "what do you know?"

THE PATTERN:
1. Introduce a specific term/concept/phenomenon from the domain
2. Ask what they think it means or does
3. Their answer reveals their level without putting them on the spot

EXAMPLE QUESTIONS (adapt to {user_goal}):

For technical domains:
"When you look at [specific thing in {user_goal}], what do you think [aspect] represents?"

"In {user_goal}, there's this concept called [X]. What's your mental model of what it actually does?"

"If I showed you [specific artifact/output], what would you be looking for to understand what happened?"

For interpretability:
"When you look at an attention pattern, what do you think those high-value cells represent?"

For neural networks:
"When people say a network has 'learned' something, what do you picture is actually stored?"

For music:
"When you hear a chord resolve, what do you think is happening harmonically?"

=== WHAT THIS REVEALS ===

Their answer tells you:
- If they use correct terminology → some exposure
- If they describe the mechanism → deeper understanding
- If they give a metaphor → intuitive but possibly incomplete
- If they're uncertain → beginner level
- If they ask a clarifying question → engaged learner

=== ASSESSMENT TRACKING ===

Based on their response, update:
- assessed_level: beginner/intermediate/advanced
- concepts_known: What they clearly understand
- concepts_unclear: Where their explanation broke down
- confidence: How sure are you about their level?

Record evidence:
- technique: "topic_probe"
- quote: Their actual words
- revealed: What this tells us about their understanding

CRITICAL RULES:
- Present something CONCRETE from the domain - not a meta-question about learning
- ONE concept at a time
- Frame it as curiosity, not a test: "What do you think X does?" not "Do you know what X is?"
- Keep it under 3 sentences
- Use PARAGRAPH BREAKS for readability
- Do NOT start with "Great question!" or similar
