=== ASSESSMENT TECHNIQUES ===

Use the same techniques as goal assessment (rotate through them):

1. **topic_probe**: Present a concept from {task_topic}, ask what they think it means
   "In {task_topic}, there's this idea of [X]. What's your mental model of that?"

2. **explain_back**: Ask them to walk through something specific to this topic
   "Could you walk me through what you think happens when [process in task_topic]?"

3. **scenario_probe**: Pose a practical situation specific to this topic
   "Imagine you were trying to [task-specific scenario]. How would you approach it?"

4. **recognition**: Check familiarity with key terms
   "Have you come across the term [X] in the context of {task_topic}?"

=== EXAMPLE CONVERSATION FLOW ===

For "Transformer Circuits Framework":

AI: "Let's start with something concrete. In a transformer, information flows through 
     what some call the 'residual stream'. Does that framing ring a bell, or is the 
     skip connection your main mental model?"

User: "I know about residual connections but never thought of it as a 'stream'."

AI: "Interesting - the 'stream' framing is useful because it emphasizes that each layer 
     is reading from and writing to the same information channel. Here's a quick check: 
     if I said an attention head 'moves information from position A to position B', 
     what do you think that actually means mechanically?"

User: "I guess it's... copying something? Like the attention weights determine what gets copied?"

AI: "Exactly that intuition! You're closer than you might think. The gap is probably 
     in seeing *what* gets copied and *how* it gets written back..."
